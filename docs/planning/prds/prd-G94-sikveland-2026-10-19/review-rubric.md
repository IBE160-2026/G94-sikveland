# PRD Quality Review — Norway Visa Guide For Thai

*Reviewed 2026-11-02 against `prd-validation-checklist.md`. Stakes: IBE160 course project; the goal is a working prototype. Rigor is set to "usable by downstream UX, architecture and stories." The FR/NFR IDs carried over from `epics-and-stories.md` are a deliberate choice and are not treated as defects.*

## Overall verdict
This is a focused PRD built around real journeys. Fah and Michael drive actual requirements. The glossary is disciplined, scope and non-goals are honest, and the course-time metric (SM-2) is tied to a concrete test set. The risk sits in a few FRs that downstream work will struggle to build or test as written. FR6 never says whether the checklist is fixed content or generated by the model, and saved ticks only work if it is fixed. FR9's "delete personal information, then answer the rest" contradicts the classifier design in the addendum and cannot be met for "full address." A handful of ACs (source links on every answer, "correct" in NFR4, NFR1 adjectives) need tightening before they become stories.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions. Some examples: "Only the Yes/No answer is stored, not the conversation" (FR5), "Ticks are not stored on the server" (FR6), the fixed step order in FR12, and hosting moved out of scope "for the course" (§8.2). Several entries in §8.2 say what was given up and why ("not needed; checklist ticks stay on the phone"; "sources are updated by script"). That is honest trade-off writing.

The gaps sit where the PRD meets its own constraints. The single open question (§9, "Project deadline and group size … affects how much of §8.1 fits") is genuinely open. But the PRD gives no cut order if §8.1 doesn't fit, so the decision it defers can't be made from the document. The PRD also has no `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts, even though at least one real tension exists (FR3, below).

### Findings
- **medium** No priority order for MVP scope under the open deadline question (§8.1, §9) — §9 says the deadline "affects how much of §8.1 fits," but §8.1 is a flat list of nine items. Nobody can tell whether FR7 (English translation), FR11's update script or FR6 goes first if time runs short. *Fix:* Mark §8.1 items as must/should (e.g. must: FR1, FR2, FR3, FR4, FR10, FR12; should: FR6, FR7, FR11 script, FR5). Alternatively, add a one-line cut order under §9.
- **low** FR3 wording may undercut the adviser's own business (§4.2 FR3, §8.2) — "the decision is made by the embassy and that no one else can answer it" is true about the visa decision. But §8.2 lists "a paid service for personal guidance" as long-term vision, which the adviser would sell. The wording is a deliberate user decision (memlog), so this is a note, not a defect. *Fix:* Add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` asking whether FR3 should also offer the adviser contact, or keep the wording and record why.

## Substance over theater — strong
Personas earn their place. Fah's Thai-only reading drives FR1 and the Thai source note in UJ-1. Her return visits drive FR6 tick persistence. Her edge case drives the sponsorship-form clause in FR6/FR11. Michael's dialogue drives FR5's Yes/No loop, FR13 and the relationship-proof coverage in FR11. The Vision is specific to this product: it names the adviser, the Facebook entry and the night/weekend email wait. Most NFRs carry product-specific thresholds (NFR4's 90%/100%, NFR5's exact storage list, NFR8's enumerated fixed texts). Only one line reads like furniture.

### Findings
- **low** Secondary users drive nothing (§2.1) — "members of the public who want to learn about these visas without planning to apply now" appears once and shapes no FR, UJ or metric. *Fix:* Delete it, or add one clause stating it implies no extra requirement (e.g. "served by the same chat; no separate features").

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear and carried through: Thai applicants can prepare the application themselves if they get ordered, source-backed Thai answers and a checklist, and that frees the adviser from repeated questions. The features follow from it: steps (FR12), checklist (FR6), honest limits (FR3/FR4/FR14). The scope kind (problem-solving) fits. SM-C1 is a real counter-metric that guards SM-1 against vanity. Splitting metrics into "during the course" and "after launch" is a good call given that hosting is out of scope.

Two weaknesses remain. SM-1 cannot be computed as defined. And the PRD's strongest claim, that the checklist replaces the adviser's document check (UJ-1 Resolution: "does not need the adviser to check her documents"), is not validated by any metric.

### Findings
- **medium** SM-1 is not measurable from the data the PRD allows to be stored (§6 SM-1, FR5, NFR5) — SM-1 is "Share of 'Yes' among applicants." But NFR5 stores only "the Yes/No answer … and its time," with no way to group answers by conversation. FR5's loop also means a finished conversation always ends in "Yes" after any number of "No"s. So a per-answer ratio undercounts, and a per-conversation ratio cannot be computed. Applicants who never give a done signal are invisible. *Fix:* Define the unit, e.g. "share of conversations whose *first* feedback answer is Yes." Then allow storing an anonymous random conversation ID with each answer (add it to NFR5).
- **medium** The checklist's core claim has no metric (§6, §4.3, UJ-1 Resolution) — FR6 is the feature meant to "replace asking the adviser to check the documents," yet neither SM-1 nor SM-2 lists FR6, and NFR4's test set does not check checklist content. *Fix:* Extend SM-C1 to also count "please check my documents" emails. As a course-time check, add to NFR4 that each visit-purpose checklist is compared item by item against UDI's checklist and approved by the adviser.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Several FRs are well bounded: FR10 (three checkable ACs), FR12 (explicit order), FR5 (Yes/No branches), FR1 (typos and mixed language), FR11 (publisher allow-list, 10–20 sources, required coverage topics). NFR4 is unusually concrete for a course project.

The problems cluster in two FRs that will become stories with no clear "done" (FR6, FR9), plus one AC that contradicts other FRs (FR2). FR7, FR8 and FR13 have no ACs at all. NFR1 uses adjectives where bounds are needed, and response time has no bound anywhere.

### Findings
- **high** FR6 does not say where checklist content comes from, so tick persistence is under-specified (§4.3 FR6, §3 Checklist, NFR8) — Ticks "kept between visits" only work if each checklist item has a stable identity. That requires curated, fixed checklist content per visit purpose, not a model-generated answer. The PRD never says which. NFR8's list of "fixed Thai texts" omits checklists, which suggests they are generated. UJ-1 step 3 has the checklist arriving as a chat answer, while FR6 says the applicant "can select a visit purpose," so the UX shape (chat message vs. separate checklist view) is also open. The set of visit purposes is only "e.g. visiting family" (§3), with §8.1 implying tourism plus visiting family/friends. *Fix:* Add ACs to FR6. (a) Checklists are fixed content, one per visit purpose, stored with the knowledge base and covered by NFR8 review. (b) Visit purposes for MVP are listed explicitly (e.g. tourism; visiting family; visiting friends). (c) Say how the applicant reaches it, e.g. "a chat answer about documents offers a 'Open checklist' button; the checklist view lets the applicant pick a visit purpose." (d) If a checklist changes, ticks on items that remain are kept.
- **high** FR9 contradicts the addendum's classifier, and "full address" cannot be deleted as specified (§4.5 FR9, §3 Personal information, addendum "Question classifier" / "Personal information deletion") — FR9 says "The chatbot still answers the rest of the question." But the addendum routes `personal_data` questions away from answering ("Only `general` goes on to the answering step"). The addendum's pattern filter covers "passport, ID and phone numbers" but not full addresses, which the glossary includes. Addresses can't be reliably caught by patterns. The classifier is itself an AI-service call, so an address would reach the AI service before any detection. That breaks "deleted before the question is sent to the AI service." *Fix:* In FR9, limit the "deleted before sending" guarantee to pattern-detectable items (Thai ID number, passport number, phone number). Treat full address as best-effort (warned, not guaranteed), or drop it from the glossary definition. Then fix the addendum: the filter strips personal data first, the cleaned question goes through the normal classifier, and the reminder is added to whatever answer results. `personal_data` is no longer a terminal category.
- **medium** "Each answer shows at least one source link" conflicts with the limit answers (§4.1 FR2 vs FR3, FR4, FR13, FR14, FR5) — The no-answer reply (FR4) by definition has no source, and the embassy-decides, out-of-scope, reach-a-person and feedback messages are fixed texts. An AC applied literally to "each answer" makes those fail tests. *Fix:* Scope FR2 to "each answer to a general question (one answered from the knowledge base)," and state that FR3/FR4/FR13/FR14/FR5 replies are fixed texts with no source link (they are covered by NFR8).
- **medium** "Correct answer" in NFR4 and SM-2 has no judging rule (§5 NFR4, §6 SM-2) — "at least 90% of general questions get a correct answer with the correct source" is the only metric testable in the course, but it doesn't say who decides "correct" or how. It also doesn't say what share of the 20–30 questions are "general." Out-of-scope and personal-data questions are in the test set but have no pass rule. *Fix:* Add to NFR4: (a) a question is "correct" if it contains the key facts listed in the expected answer, adds no facts outside the sources, and cites the expected source, judged by a group member and logged per question; (b) at least 15 general questions; (c) out-of-scope questions must get the FR14 reply and personal-data questions must show the FR9 deletion, both at 100%.
- **medium** NFR1 is adjectives, not bounds (§5 NFR1) — "works well on a phone," "large touch targets," "simple language" cannot be tested. *Fix:* E.g. "Usable at 360 px width with no horizontal scrolling; touch targets at least 44×44 px; checked in Facebook's in-app browser on one Android and one iOS phone (when hosted, see §8.2) and in mobile Chrome/Safari locally."
- **medium** No response-time bound (§5) — The addendum design makes two Opus calls per question (classifier and answer) with the whole knowledge base attached. On a phone this can take many seconds, and nothing says what is acceptable or what the applicant sees while waiting. *Fix:* Add an NFR such as "A typing/loading indicator appears within 1 s; 90% of answers arrive within 15 s on the test set." Let architecture choose a cheaper classifier model if needed.
- **low** FR7, FR8, FR13 have no ACs (§4.1, §4.2) — FR7 doesn't say what gets translated (answer text only? source titles?) or whether translation is a new model call that counts toward NFR6 cost. FR8 doesn't say how many example questions there are or that they match demo mode. FR13 doesn't say how a "request for a person" is recognized. *Fix:* FR7: "A 'Show in English' button under each answer shows an English version of that answer; source links are unchanged." FR8: "4–6 example questions, the same set answered in demo mode (FR10) and reviewed under NFR8." FR13: add a Thai and English example phrase that must trigger it, and put both in the test set.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§7 Non-Goals does real work: no eligibility assessment, no tailored advice, no form filling. §8.2 states its omissions with reasons, including the important admission that "24-hour availability and the Facebook link in UJ-1 come when it is hosted." Both inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed in §10. The open-items count (1 open question, 2 assumptions, 0 PM notes) fits the stakes.

Two omissions are left for the reader to infer. First, the Vision and UJ-1 promise things the MVP won't deliver. Second, several kept FR IDs now mean something different from the stories that already cite them.

### Findings
- **medium** Kept IDs changed meaning, and the PRD doesn't say so (§0, §4 intro, `epics-and-stories.md`) — §0 says IDs are kept "so stories still trace." But `epics-and-stories.md` still defines FR3 as "referred to a human adviser," FR5 as asked "After an answer," and FR9 as warn-only. The PRD has reversed or extended all three. A story writer who trusts the shared IDs will build the old behavior. (The memlog notes the stories are stale; the PRD itself does not.) *Fix:* Add a short "Changed since epics-and-stories.md" note under §4's intro listing FR3, FR5, FR6 (now in MVP, with saved ticks), FR9 and NFR4/NFR5, and stating that the PRD wins.
- **medium** Vision and UJ-1 describe a hosted product, and NFR1's Facebook in-app browser check can't be done locally (§1, §2.3 UJ-1 Entry state, §5 NFR1, §8.2) — The Vision says "answers … 24 hours a day" and "reached from the adviser's Facebook page." UJ-1's entry is the Facebook link. NFR1 requires the in-app browser. But §8.2 defers hosting. The PRD never says whether NFR1's in-app-browser clause is an MVP acceptance condition. *Fix:* In NFR1, mark the in-app-browser clause `[NON-GOAL for MVP]: verified when hosted`, or add a cheap verification path (e.g. a temporary tunnel link opened from Facebook Messenger). Add one sentence to §1 or §0 saying the course MVP runs locally and in demo mode.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The glossary is a real asset: 18 terms, used consistently ("adviser contact," "individual-assessment question," "done signal," "sponsor" vs. the old "host"). Both UJs have named protagonists with inline context. FR→UJ "Realizes" links resolve, and the addendum cleanly separates tech notes for architecture from the verbatim dialogue for UX.

The weak spots are a few concepts used but never defined, and the addendum/PRD contradiction already raised under FR9.

### Findings
- **medium** "Conversation" is used throughout but never defined (§3, FR1, FR5, NFR5) — FR1 needs context "earlier in the same conversation," FR5 "ends the conversation," and NFR5 says "Conversation text is not stored." Architecture and UX need to know where a conversation starts and ends: a page reload? A new tab? After "Yes"? And where its context lives, given that nothing is stored on the server. *Fix:* Add a glossary entry, e.g. "**Conversation** — The exchange on one open chat page, from opening until 'Yes' to the feedback question or until the page is closed or reloaded. Held only in the browser and sent with each question; never stored on the server." Also define what the UI does after "Yes" (e.g. input disabled, "Start new conversation" button).
- **low** FR11's adviser review and per-answer traceability have no mechanism (§4.6 FR11) — "the adviser reviews the changes before they are used" needs a channel, since §8.2 rules out an admin page and the adviser is not described as technical. "Every answer can be traced to a specific version of a source" needs to say where the trace is visible, since conversations are not stored. *Fix:* E.g. "The script writes a summary of changed sources; a group member shows it to the adviser, and the new version is used only after the adviser approves (recorded in the commit message)." And: "Each source link in an answer shows the source's retrieval date."

## Shape fit — strong
This is a consumer product with real UX stakes (Thai-only users, mobile, Facebook entry), and it feeds UX, architecture and stories. Journeys with named protagonists are load-bearing here, and both UJs pull their weight. The PRD is about five pages, which suits a course project, and implementation detail went to the addendum rather than into FRs. The user deliberately chose not to write an adviser-side journey. That is reasonable, but it is the reason FR11's review step and NFR8's review gate are thin on mechanism (see the FR11 finding).

### Findings
- **low** UJ-2 lacks the Entry state and Edge case fields that UJ-1 has (§2.3 UJ-2) — For UX source extraction, the two journeys should have the same shape. *Fix:* Add "Entry state: same as UJ-1" and either an edge case (e.g. Michael asks "Will I get the visa?" → FR3), or "Edge case: none."

  - "general questions" (NFR4) matches the addendum's classifier label but is not a glossary term. Define it as "a question that is not individual-assessment, out-of-scope or personal-information."
  - "agency email address" (UJ-2 step 3) should be "adviser contact" according to the glossary.
- **Section structure.** §4.1–4.4 have a **Description** line, but §4.5–4.7 do not. §4.1 has a "Functional Requirements:" label that the other sections lack.

- **Cross-refs.** `docs/planning/product-brief.md` and `docs/planning/epics-and-stories.md` exist. UJ-1's "(see NFR1)" resolves. FR9 → NFR5 and NFR4 → NFR3 references resolve.
